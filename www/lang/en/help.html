CamWall – help

CamWall shows the picture from every IP camera listed in cameras.yaml on one page, together with the temperature and humidity from the TH16-R2 weather station and its sensors, configured in th16/th16_config.json. Camera video never leaves your network – no cloud, no account. Only station readings that cannot be read locally are fetched from Tuya Cloud.

Step-by-step instructions for setting up the cameras and the weather station are in README.md in the CamWall folder.

Features

  • All cameras on one screen. The grid adapts to the window size; on a phone the cameras are stacked.
  • Group filters at the top (e.g. Outside / Inside), taken from the group field in cameras.yaml.
  • Status of every camera: Live, Connecting or Offline with the reason (e.g. camera unreachable, wrong password).
  • Automatic reconnect when the connection drops or the picture freezes.
  • Count of live cameras and a clock in the top bar.
  • Temperature and humidity from the TH16-R2 weather station and its wireless sensors in the top bar (see Weather station below).
  • Language versions with a switch in the top bar (e.g. PL | EN). Your choice is remembered.
  • Streams pause while the browser tab is hidden, sparing the cameras and the network.
  • Passwords stay in the .env file and the weather station keys in the th16 folder; they never reach the browser.

Using the page

  • The wall shows every camera in low resolution (its sub stream, rtsp_substream_url), sparing the cameras and the network. Double-click (on a phone: double-tap) a camera, or press Enter, to enlarge it to the full window in high resolution (rtsp_url). A camera without rtsp_substream_url is shown in high resolution on the wall as well.
  • When enlarged: ← → switch to the previous or next camera; double-click, Esc or ✕ closes it.
  • Click the temperatures in the top bar (or press W) for details: humidity, sensor battery and the time of each reading.
  • F or the ⛶ button toggles full screen, ? opens this help, the ⓘ button shows information about the program.
  • The Add camera tile explains how to add a camera.

Running

  • Windows: double-click start.bat.
  • Raspberry Pi: bash start.sh in the cam_wall folder, or use autostart (see below).
  • Then open http://localhost:1984, or http://<computer IP>:1984 from another device on the network.
  • Keep the CamWall window open. Ctrl+C stops it.
  • After editing cameras.yaml, .env or th16/th16_config.json, restart CamWall.
  • Useful commands: python camwall.py --check (checks the configuration and translations, and reads the weather station once), --download (downloads go2rtc), --help (help in the terminal).
  • Page texts and translations are in the www/lang folder; see www/lang/README.txt.

Weather station

  • The station's files are in the th16 folder. How to create them (Tuya IoT account, TinyTuya wizard): README.md, section 2. Configure the weather station.
  • CamWall reads the main station and the wireless sensors paired with it every minute over the home network. Anything that does not answer locally is read from Tuya Cloud instead, at most every 5 minutes.
  • The time of each reading is shown in the details; a faded value means the reading is old (the station or sensor has not answered for a while).
  • Station IP address, names and refresh times: th16/th16_config.json. Turn the display on or off in cameras.yaml, settings section: weather: auto (default: shown if th16/devices.json exists), on or off.
  • A dash “–” or faded values: check station_ip in th16/th16_config.json (the station's address can change after a router restart – reserve it in the router's DHCP settings), then run python camwall.py --check, which shows where each reading came from.

Access from outside home (optional)

  • Install Tailscale (tailscale.com) on the CamWall computer and on your phone or laptop, and sign in to the same account on both.
  • CamWall detects Tailscale when it starts and shows the address in its window, e.g. http://camwall-pc.….ts.net:1984. Open that address when away from home.
  • Without Tailscale CamWall works as before, on the home network only. The cameras do not need Tailscale.
  • Setting in cameras.yaml, settings section: tailscale: auto (default: use it if present), on (always, warn if it is not running) or off (never).

Installation – Windows 10/11

  1. Install Python 3 from python.org and tick “Add python.exe to PATH”.
  2. Copy the whole CamWall folder, including the .env file and the th16 folder.
  3. Run start.bat. The first time it installs PyYAML and TinyTuya and offers to download go2rtc – answer Y.
  4. Allow go2rtc through Windows Firewall (private networks).

Installation – Raspberry Pi

  1. Copy the whole CamWall folder (including .env and th16) to the Pi as ~/cam_wall (USB stick or scp -r cam_wall_with_temp_sensor user@Pi-IP:~/cam_wall).
  2. In a terminal: cd ~/cam_wall, then bash start.sh. It installs python3-yaml and TinyTuya and downloads go2rtc for the Pi – answer Y.
  3. Start automatically on boot: bash install-service.sh
    logs: journalctl -u camwall -f
    restart after config changes: sudo systemctl restart camwall
    remove autostart: bash install-service.sh --remove
  4. Camera wall on a monitor connected to the Pi: chromium-browser --kiosk http://localhost:1984
